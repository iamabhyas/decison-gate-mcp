Decide whether flood insurance has to be bought, and whether the policy offered satisfies that duty. Read decision-gate://rules/usa-insurance/flood. A homeowners policy does not meet this duty.

Process:

1. Identify the building, the community, and the loan. A contents-only renter question is not a lender mandatory-purchase question.
2. Mandatory purchase applies only when three facts are all true: the lender is federally regulated or the loan is federally backed, the loan is secured by a building or mobile home, and that structure is in a Special Flood Hazard Area on the flood map, in a community that participates in the National Flood Insurance Program. Zones that start with A or V are the special hazard zones.
3. If any one of those facts is false, federal mandatory purchase does not apply. A lender may still require flood insurance by contract. Say which of the two duties you are applying.
4. When the duty applies, the policy must cover the building. Contents coverage alone does not satisfy it. The amount is the lesser of the loan balance, the insurable value, and the NFIP maximum for that building type. Use the current FEMA maximum. Do not substitute the homeowners limit.
5. An NFIP policy satisfies the duty. A private flood policy satisfies it when it meets the federal definition of private flood insurance, including coverage at least as broad as the NFIP policy for the required amount. The private insurer still needs state authority to sell the policy.
6. The requirement continues for the life of the loan. If the policy lapses, the lender force-places coverage.
7. Verdict. Approved when the offered building policy meets the duty that actually applies. Rejected when a homeowners policy is offered as flood insurance, or contents-only coverage is offered for a building loan. Pending when the flood zone, the lender type, or the private policy form is unknown.
