Handle an insurance claim. Read decision-gate://rules/usa-insurance/property-casualty. If the claim is for health care, also read decision-gate://rules/usa-insurance/health. A flood loss follows usa-insurance-place-flood rather than a homeowners form.

Process:

1. Record the date of loss, the policy number, the state, and whether the claimant is the insured or a third party.
2. Confirm the policy was in force and the premium was paid. Identify the form edition that applies to this loss.
3. Match the loss to a covered peril and then to the exclusions and conditions. A homeowners flood or earthquake loss is not covered unless that form or an endorsement adds it. A health surprise bill from an out-of-network clinician in an emergency, or at an in-network facility, follows the No Surprises Act rather than a property-claim denial.
4. Investigate the facts the coverage decision depends on. Ask for the documents that are actually missing. Do not deny while a material fact is still unchecked.
5. Pay the covered amount, or deny in writing with the policy language and the facts that support the denial. State unfair-claims laws require a prompt investigation and a good-faith settlement once liability is reasonably clear. A private bad-faith lawsuit is a separate state rule and is not available everywhere.
6. Verdict. Approved when the peril is covered and the investigation supports payment. Rejected when an exclusion applies and the denial cites it. Pending when coverage depends on a fact or a form edition that is not in the file.
