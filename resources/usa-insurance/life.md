# Life, annuity, and long-term care

Related index: `decision-gate://rules/usa-insurance`. These products are state-regulated insurance. Health-only rules such as GINA's ban on genetic underwriting do not automatically apply here. See `decision-gate://rules/usa-insurance/health`.

## Policy provisions states commonly require

State insurance codes set mandatory provisions for life policies issued in that state. The common pattern, which must still be checked against the issuing state's statute, is:

- A free-look period after delivery, often 10 days, and often 30 days for replacements or for buyers above a stated age. The buyer can return the policy for a refund.
- An incontestability clause, commonly two years from issue. After that, the insurer cannot void the policy for misstatements in the application. Whether fraud remains contestable after two years depends on that state's statute.
- A suicide limitation, commonly two years. Death by suicide inside the period is often limited to a return of premium.

Group life conversion rights, grace periods, and nonforfeiture values are also state policy-form requirements. The contract controls once it has been approved for use in that state.

## Replacement and illustrations

Replacing an existing life policy or annuity can cause new surrender charges and a new contestability period. States that have adopted the NAIC life-insurance and annuities replacement model require the producer to identify the replacement, give the applicant a comparison notice, and notify the existing insurer. Twisting, a misleading replacement, is an unfair practice. See `decision-gate://rules/usa-insurance/state-regulation`.

Illustrated values that are not guaranteed must follow the illustration rules of the state, which are commonly based on the NAIC life-insurance illustrations model. A decision should not treat a non-guaranteed illustrated rate as a promised benefit.

## Annuity recommendations

Many states have adopted the NAIC suitability model, as revised to a best-interest standard for annuity recommendations. In those states a producer must collect the consumer's financial profile, disclose the producer's role and compensation structure, and only recommend an annuity that is in the consumer's best interest. States that have not adopted that version may still use an older suitability standard. Apply the rule in force in the state of the sale.

## Long-term care and disability

Long-term-care insurance is state-regulated. Most states have adopted a version of the NAIC long-term-care model, including outline-of-coverage delivery, an inflation-protection offer, and a free look that is often 30 days. Partnership policies that interact with Medicaid spend-down rules must meet additional federal and state conditions.

Disability income insurance is state-regulated and is not an ACA essential-health-benefit policy. Preexisting-condition limits, benefit periods, and the definition of disability are contract terms within that state's form rules.

Life, annuity, disability, and long-term-care claims against an insolvent admitted insurer may fall to the state life-and-health guaranty association, up to that state's statutory cap.
