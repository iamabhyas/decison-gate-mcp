# Privacy and consumer reports

Related index: `decision-gate://rules/usa-insurance`. These rules sit on top of state insurance regulation. They are not the same as a coverage or claim rule.

## HIPAA

The HIPAA Privacy, Security, and Breach Notification Rules apply to covered entities and their business associates. Health plans, including health insurers, HMOs, and employer group health plans, are covered entities. Healthcare clearinghouses and providers who conduct standard electronic transactions are covered entities too.

HIPAA does not govern life insurance, workers' compensation, or a typical property and casualty policy. Using a HIPAA authorization or a privacy-rule standard for those lines is the wrong framework unless a health-plan transaction is actually involved.

A covered health plan may use and disclose protected health information for treatment, payment, and healthcare operations. Marketing, sale of information, and many other disclosures need a written authorization. Individuals have rights to access, amend, and receive an accounting of certain disclosures. A breach of unsecured protected health information requires notice to affected individuals and, above the federal thresholds, to the Department of Health and Human Services and sometimes the media.

## Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act

Insurers are financial institutions under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act. The state insurance department enforces the insurance privacy rules, usually through a version of the NAIC privacy model.

- The insurer gives customers a privacy notice describing what nonpublic personal information it collects and shares.
- A customer can opt out of sharing with nonaffiliated third parties for those third parties' own marketing.
- Sharing to service the policy, to complete a transaction the consumer requested, or under a joint-marketing agreement that meets the rule does not require that opt-out.
- The annual-notice requirement has a federal exception when the insurer's sharing has not changed and it does not share in a way that triggers an opt-out.

The insurer must also keep an information-security program. Many states have adopted the NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law, which requires a written security program, investigation of a cybersecurity event, and notice to the commissioner. Adoption is not universal. Use the law of the licensee's state.

## Fair Credit Reporting Act

When an insurer uses a consumer report, including a credit-based insurance score or a report from a consumer-reporting agency, the Fair Credit Reporting Act applies.

- If the report is a factor in an adverse underwriting decision, such as a decline, a higher rate, or a less favorable tier, the insurer must give an adverse-action notice. The notice identifies the consumer-reporting agency and states that the agency did not make the decision.
- Using a consumer report requires a permissible purpose. Insurance underwriting is a permissible purpose when the person is involved in that underwriting decision.
- Medical information in a consumer report has additional restrictions.

State insurance codes can be stricter than the Fair Credit Reporting Act on the use of credit in personal-lines rating, including bans or extraordinary-life-circumstance adjustments. A federal permissible purpose does not override a state ban on credit-based insurance scoring.
