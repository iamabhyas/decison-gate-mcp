# Flood insurance

Related index: `decision-gate://rules/usa-insurance`. A homeowners decision that treats flood as a covered peril is addressed in `decision-gate://rules/usa-insurance/property-casualty`.

## The program

The National Flood Insurance Program was created by the National Flood Insurance Act of 1968 and is administered by FEMA. Communities participate by adopting floodplain-management rules. People and businesses in participating communities can buy an NFIP policy, subject to FEMA's underwriting and the program's maximum limits.

NFIP building and contents limits are set by federal rule and differ for one-to-four-family homes, other residential buildings, and non-residential buildings. Use the current FEMA maximum for the building type. Do not substitute a homeowners limit.

A standard homeowners, renters, or commercial-property policy excludes flood. Flood coverage is a separate NFIP policy or a private flood policy.

## Mandatory purchase

The Flood Disaster Protection Act of 1973, as later amended, requires flood insurance when all of the following are true:

- a lender is federally regulated, or the loan is government-sponsored or backed by a federal agency
- the loan is secured by a building or mobile home
- that structure is in a Special Flood Hazard Area, mapped as a zone beginning with A or V, in a participating community

The amount must be at least the lesser of the outstanding principal balance, the insurable value of the structure, or the maximum available through the NFIP. Contents-only coverage does not satisfy the building requirement. The requirement lasts for the life of the loan. The lender, not the insurance department, forces placement if the borrower lets the policy lapse.

Private flood insurance may satisfy the mandatory-purchase requirement when it meets the federal definition of private flood insurance, including coverage at least as broad as the NFIP policy for the required amount. A lender may accept a private policy that does not meet that definition only within the limited discretion federal guidance allows. A private policy that is narrower than the NFIP form should not be treated as automatic compliance.

## What this rule does not do

- It does not require flood insurance on a property outside the Special Flood Hazard Area, though a lender may still require it by contract.
- It does not require flood insurance for a cash purchase with no federally related loan.
- It does not replace state regulation of a private flood insurer's license and forms. The private carrier still needs state authority to write the policy. See `decision-gate://rules/usa-insurance/state-regulation`.
