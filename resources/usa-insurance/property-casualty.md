# Property, casualty, and workers' compensation

Related index: `decision-gate://rules/usa-insurance`. Flood on a mortgaged building is a separate federal rule: `decision-gate://rules/usa-insurance/flood`.

## Automobile financial responsibility

Liability insurance, or another form of financial responsibility the state accepts, is compulsory for private passenger autos in nearly every state. New Hampshire is the long-standing exception that does not require a liability policy before driving, and it still requires drivers who cause a crash to show they can pay, up to that state's financial-responsibility limits.

Each state sets its own minimum bodily-injury and property-damage limits, and decides whether uninsured-motorist or personal-injury-protection coverage is mandatory. Those numbers are not national. A decision that uses another state's minimums, or a remembered national minimum, is not supported.

Proof of insurance is enforced at registration and during traffic stops under that state's motor-vehicle law. Driving without the required security can mean a citation, suspension, and personal liability. The insurance policy's terms still control what the insurer pays.

## Homeowners and other property

No general statute requires a homeowner to buy a homeowners policy. A mortgage lender or landlord usually requires the policy by contract, and the lender is often named as mortgagee.

A standard homeowners form is not flood insurance and is not earthquake insurance unless an endorsement or a separate policy adds that peril. Wind and hurricane deductibles are policy terms and, in coastal states, are constrained by state form rules.

## Claims handling

Most states have an unfair-claims-settlement law based on the NAIC model. Typical duties of the insurer are to:

- acknowledge and investigate claims promptly
- not misrepresent relevant policy language
- explain a denial with reference to the facts and the policy
- attempt in good faith to settle a claim where liability has become reasonably clear

The model act itself often gives enforcement to the insurance department and does not create a private lawsuit. Separate bad-faith statutes and court decisions do, and they differ by state and by whether the claim is first-party or third-party. Do not assume a private bad-faith remedy exists in every state.

## Workers' compensation

Workers' compensation is compulsory for most private employers in almost every state. The employer insures with an admitted carrier, a state fund where one exists, or, if the state allows it, self-insures with approval. Benefits are set by statute: medical care and a wage-replacement schedule, in exchange for the employee's ordinary negligence suit against the employer.

Texas generally allows a private employer to opt out of subscribing. An opt-out employer loses the exclusive-remedy bar and certain common-law defenses. Other states' exemptions are narrower, often for very small employers, farm labor, or domestic employees. Confirm the threshold in the state where the employee works.
