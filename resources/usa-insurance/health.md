# Health coverage rules

Related index: `decision-gate://rules/usa-insurance`. State licensing and form rules still apply to a health insurer: `decision-gate://rules/usa-insurance/state-regulation`. Privacy of health-plan data is in `decision-gate://rules/usa-insurance/privacy`.

Decide first whether the arrangement is an insurance policy, a self-funded employer plan, or a public program. The federal overlay is different for each.

## Affordable Care Act market rules

For non-grandfathered individual and group health insurance, and for group health plans, the ACA market rules in the Public Health Service Act generally include:

- No preexisting-condition exclusions, and no denial or rating based on health status in the individual and small-group markets.
- Guaranteed issue and guaranteed renewability, with listed exceptions.
- Children may stay on a parent's plan until age 26.
- No annual or lifetime dollar limits on essential health benefits.
- Essential health benefits, at the state's benchmark, for individual and small-group coverage. Large-group and self-funded plans are not required to cover the essential-health-benefit package, but they cannot impose annual or lifetime dollar limits on benefits that are essential health benefits.
- Specified preventive services with no cost sharing in non-grandfathered plans.
- A medical loss ratio of at least 80 percent in the individual and small-group markets and 85 percent in the large-group market, with rebates if the insurer falls short.

Excepted benefits are outside many of these rules. Standalone dental and vision, specified-disease, hospital indemnity, and similar policies are excepted benefits only when they meet the federal conditions. Do not apply major-medical rules to a policy that is actually excepted.

An applicable large employer, generally 50 or more full-time employees including full-time equivalents, can owe an employer shared-responsibility payment if it does not offer affordable minimum-value coverage and an employee receives a marketplace subsidy. The payment amount is indexed. Confirm the current figure before using a dollar threshold.

## ERISA and state benefit mandates

ERISA governs most private-employer benefit plans.

- ERISA preempts state laws that relate to an employee benefit plan.
- State insurance law is saved, so the insurance contract behind an insured plan remains subject to state mandates and the state insurance department.
- A self-funded employer plan is not deemed to be an insurance company. State insurance benefit mandates generally do not apply to the self-funded plan. Federal rules such as ACA market reforms, COBRA, HIPAA, and parity still can.

A decision that imposes a state mandate on a self-funded ERISA plan, or that ignores state mandates on the insurer's contract, has the structure backwards.

## COBRA

Federal COBRA applies to group health plans of employers that had 20 or more employees on a typical business day in the prior calendar year. A qualifying event such as termination of employment or reduction in hours generally allows up to 18 months of continuation. Certain events, including death, divorce, and a dependent losing eligibility, can allow up to 36 months. The election period is 60 days. The plan may charge up to 102 percent of the cost of coverage.

Many states have a "mini-COBRA" law for smaller employers or longer continuation. Check that state when the employer is under the federal threshold.

## Parity, surprise bills, and genetic information

- The Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act requires financial requirements and treatment limits on mental-health and substance-use benefits to be no more restrictive than those on medical and surgical benefits, when the plan offers those benefits. Individual and small-group plans that must cover those essential health benefits are also subject to parity.
- The No Surprises Act, effective in 2022, limits patient balance bills for emergency services, for certain non-emergency services by out-of-network clinicians at in-network facilities, and for air ambulance. Plans and out-of-network providers resolve payment through the federal independent dispute process unless a state process applies. Uninsured and self-pay patients have good-faith estimate rights.
- GINA Title I bars health insurers and group health plans from using genetic information for eligibility, premiums, or preexisting conditions. It does not impose that same ban on life, disability, or long-term-care insurance.

## Medicare and Medicaid

Medicare is federal health coverage, administered by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, for people 65 and older and for younger people who qualify through disability, ALS, or end-stage renal disease. Part A is hospital insurance, Part B is medical insurance, Part C is Medicare Advantage through private plans, and Part D is drug coverage. Coordination with employer coverage depends on the employer's size and whether Medicare is primary.

Medicaid is a joint federal-state program. Eligibility, benefits, and whether the state expanded coverage to adults under 138 percent of the federal poverty level are state choices within federal rules. Do not assume one state's Medicaid eligibility applies in another.
